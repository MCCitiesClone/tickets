# Ticket lifecycle

A ticket moves through **open → (claim) → reply → (close request) → close →
transcript**. This page walks each stage. The commands referenced here are
listed in full on the [slash-command reference](/docs/commands).

## Opening a ticket

A member clicks a panel's button (or picks an option from a
[multi-panel](/docs/panels#multi-panels)). Before the channel is created, the
bot checks, in order:

1. **Panel enabled** — a disabled panel refuses new tickets.
2. **Category configured** — the panel's category override, else the guild's
   ticket category, must exist.
3. **Access control** — the panel's allow/deny role rules (first match wins;
   empty = everyone allowed).
4. **Per-user limit** — the guild `ticket limit` (staff are exempt).
5. **Cooldown** — the panel's per-user cooldown, if set (staff are exempt).

If the panel has [form questions](/docs/panels#panel-forms), a Discord modal is
shown first and the answers are stored on the ticket.

The bot then:

- assigns the next per-guild ticket **number** from an atomic counter,
- creates a **private channel** under the category — `@everyone` denied, opener
  and staff roles allowed — named from the naming scheme (`{number}` /
  `{username}`),
- posts the **welcome message** (the panel or guild
  [template](/docs/messages), else a built-in embed), with the form answers in
  their own embed, and
- adds the control buttons (Claim, Close, Close with reason) subject to the
  panel's button-visibility settings.

Any **mention roles** on the panel are pinged so staff are notified.

## Claiming

Staff assign a ticket to themselves with the **Claim** button or `/claim`, and
release it with **Release** or `/unclaim`. Claiming records `claimedBy`, swaps
the button between Claim and Release, and posts the claim notice (the guild
[claim template](/docs/messages) or a built-in embed).

## Status board

Point **Status board channel** in [Settings](/docs/configuration) at a channel
and the bot keeps a single message there listing every open ticket, grouped by
the category it sits in. Make the channel read-only for members — the bot only
needs to post and edit.

Each line shows the ticket's priority, a link to its channel, who opened it, who
claimed it (or *unclaimed*), which side is waiting, and how long it's been open
as a relative timestamp.

### Waiting on

Every open ticket tracks who owes the next reply, inferred from who spoke last:

- **⏳ Waiting on staff** — the opener wrote last, or the ticket is brand new.
- **💬 Waiting on user** — anyone else wrote last.

Only human messages count; the bot's own posts don't flip it, so a welcome
message or auto-close warning never makes a ticket look answered.

### How it stays current

The board refreshes on the same ~60s timer as the auto-close sweeps, and again
immediately when a ticket opens or closes — the two changes an operator most
wants to see land.

It's a single message edited in place rather than one per ticket, and a refresh
that would change nothing is skipped entirely, so a quiet server costs no Discord
calls at all. Delete the message and the next refresh posts a fresh one.

Very large queues are summarised rather than truncated: a category with more
than 20 open tickets shows the newest 20 and "…and *n* more", and the footer
always counts every open ticket, including ones not listed.


## Support hours

A server can tell members when its support team is around. When a ticket opens
**outside** those hours, the bot posts a notice in the channel: that a reply may
take longer, and when support is next available.

Configure it in [Settings](/docs/configuration):

- **Timezone** — an IANA name like `Europe/London`. Hours are stored as
  wall-clock times in this zone, so they follow daylight saving instead of
  drifting by an hour twice a year.
- **Support hours** — any number of day + start + end spans. A span must end
  after it starts; for an overnight shift add two, one ending at `23:59` and one
  starting at `00:00` the next day.
- **Expected response time** — free text like "usually within 2 hours", shown on
  every new ticket whether or not you set hours.

Leave the schedule empty to be always available: an unset schedule never flags a
ticket as out-of-hours, so the feature stays opt-in. With neither hours nor a
hint set, no notice is posted at all.

The next-opening time is written as a Discord dynamic timestamp, so each member
reads it in **their own** timezone rather than the support team's.


## Reporting a message

Right-click any message → **Apps** → **Report message to staff** opens a ticket
about it. It's the quickest route for a member who wants to report something
offensive without having to describe where it was.

The reported message is **copied into the ticket**, not just linked: author (as
a mention, plus their name and ID at report time), when and where it was sent, a
jump link, and the text quoted. Attachments and embeds are listed by name and
count. That matters because the usual reason to report something is that it
shouldn't stay up — by the time staff read the ticket the original may already be
gone, and a link alone would point at nothing.

Everything else behaves like opening a ticket from a panel: blacklists, access
rules, cooldowns and per-user limits all apply, and the panel's form is asked if
it has one. Reporting is not a way around any of that. Reporting your own
message is refused.

### Which panel a report opens

A report has to land on a panel, since that's what decides the category, staff
roles and access rules. Resolved in this order:

1. The **Report panel** configured in [Settings](/docs/configuration).
2. The server's only panel, if there's exactly one — so a simple server needs no
   configuration at all.
3. Otherwise the reporter is asked, with an ephemeral dropdown that nobody else
   in the channel sees.

Disabled panels are never offered, and a configured panel that's since been
deleted or disabled falls back to the rules above rather than failing.


## Priority

Every ticket opens at **Normal** priority. Staff move it with
`/priority <level>` — **Low**, **Normal**, **High**, or **Urgent**. Running
`/priority` with no level shows the current one (privately) to anyone in the
ticket.

Setting a priority:

- stores it on the ticket, so it shows in the dashboard's tickets list and in
  the [open-queue-by-priority breakdown](/docs/stats#open-queue-by-priority);
- badges the channel **topic** (e.g. `🔴 URGENT · Ticket #42 · …`) so the queue
  is readable from the channel list — Normal carries no badge;
- posts a notice in the channel and a line in the [audit log](#audit-log).

Channel edits share Discord's aggressive rename rate limit, so a burst of
priority changes on one ticket may take a moment to show on the topic. The
stored priority updates immediately either way.

Turn on **Never auto-close high or urgent tickets** in
[Settings](/docs/configuration) to exempt escalated tickets from
[auto-close](#auto-close).

## Managing members

- `/add <user>` grants a member access to the ticket channel.
- `/remove <user>` revokes it.

## Renaming & switching panels

- `/rename <name>` changes the channel's name prefix while keeping the ticket
  number (e.g. `bug-42`).
- `/switchpanel <panel>` moves the ticket to a different panel, applying that
  panel's category, naming scheme, and support roles.

## Staff notes

`/notes` opens a **private thread** attached to the ticket for staff-only
discussion. The thread auto-archives after a week and the support roles are
pulled in with a "ghost ping" (a mention that is sent and immediately deleted,
so the notification fires without leaving a lingering ping). Running `/notes`
again reuses the existing thread if it's still live.

## Close requests

Rather than closing outright, a staff member can ask the opener to confirm with
`/closerequest [close_delay] [reason]`:

- `close_delay` (optional, 1–720 hours) sets an **auto-close timer** — if nobody
  responds in time, a background sweeper closes the ticket automatically.
- `reason` (optional) is attached to the request and the eventual close.

The request posts an embed with **Confirm** and **Cancel** buttons:

- **Confirm** — any participant, **including the requester**, can confirm; the
  ticket closes immediately.
- **Cancel** — the requester, the opener, or staff can cancel the pending
  request.

## Closing

A ticket closes via the **Close** button, the **Close with reason** button
(which opens a modal), `/close [reason]`, a confirmed close request, or the
auto-close timer. Closing is authorized for the opener or staff. On close the
bot:

1. **Captures history** — paginates the channel's messages into
   `ticket_message` (author snapshot, content, attachments, embeds, mentions,
   edits/deletes), on top of what was already captured live.
2. **Creates a transcript** — generates an unguessable token and a `transcript`
   record with the message count and close reason.
3. **Posts the transcript link** to the transcript channel (using the
   [transcript-post template](/docs/messages) or a built-in embed with a link
   button).
4. **DMs the opener** the link, if _DM transcript on close_ is enabled.
5. **Marks the ticket closed** (`status`, `closedAt`, `closedBy`), logs the
   action to the log channel, and **deletes the channel**.

Steps 3–4 are best-effort — a failure there won't block the ticket from
closing.

### Suggested close reasons

The same handful of reasons tend to get retyped, so the bot offers them back:

- `/close` and `/closerequest` **autocomplete** the `reason` option.
- **Close with reason** shows a **dropdown** of them above the text box. Picking
  one is optional — what you type always wins.

Suggestions come from two places, configured ones first:

1. **Close reasons** in [Settings](/docs/configuration) — a curated list, up to
   25.
2. **Recently used** reasons from this server's transcripts, so a reason staff
   keep typing becomes a suggestion on its own with no setup.

Duplicates are removed case-insensitively, and nothing is ever forced: a reason
that isn't on either list can always be typed.


## Auto-close

Enable **auto-close on inactivity** in [Settings](/docs/configuration) to close
tickets that go quiet:

- **Close after (hours)** — hours with no human message before the ticket
  auto-closes (`0` disables it). Any reply from the opener or staff resets the
  clock; the bot's own messages (welcome, warnings, canned responses) don't
  count.
- **Warn before (hours)** — post a one-time heads-up this many hours before the
  close, pinging the opener with the auto-close time (`0` skips the warning).
  Must be less than the close time.
- **Never auto-close claimed tickets** — exempt tickets a staff member has
  claimed.
- **Never auto-close high or urgent tickets** — exempt tickets staff escalated
  with [`/priority`](#priority).

A background sweep (every ~60s) checks open tickets against their guild's
threshold; auto-closed tickets go through the same close path above (recorded as
closed by the bot, reason "Auto-closed due to inactivity").

## Transcripts

Transcripts are captured two ways: **live**, as messages are sent in a tracked
ticket channel, and by a **history sweep** on close (deduplicated on the Discord
message id, so nothing is stored twice). Each transcript is viewable at a
shareable, unguessable URL — `/transcripts/<token>` — with no sign-in required,
rendering the conversation with author avatars, timestamps, attachments,
embeds, and edit/delete markers. The **Tickets** page in the dashboard lists a
server's tickets with their transcript links.

### My tickets

Members don't need Manage Server rights to find their own transcripts. Signing
in to the dashboard shows a **My tickets** page listing every ticket they opened
*or replied in*, across all servers, with a link to each transcript once the
ticket closes.

Involvement is derived from the captured messages, not from channel permissions
— an added member who never said anything isn't listed, and neither is anyone the
bot merely mentioned. The listing is scoped by the signed-in user's Discord ID in
the query itself, so it cannot surface someone else's tickets.

This changes nothing about transcript URLs: they remain shareable and
unguessable, with no sign-in required. The page only surfaces links a member is
already entitled to.

**Archived attachments:** Discord's file URLs are signed and expire, and closing
a ticket deletes its channel — so the bot copies images and files sent in a
ticket into its own storage and the transcript serves them from a token-scoped
URL, keeping them viewable long after the originals are gone. Files above the
configured size limit keep their (temporary) Discord link. See
[self-hosting](/docs/self-hosting) for the storage volume and env vars.

> **Message Content intent:** full message **bodies** require the Message
> Content privileged intent (see [Discord setup](/docs/discord-setup)). Without
> it, transcripts still record authors, attachments, and embeds, but text may be
> empty.

## Audit log

Lifecycle events are written to the configured log channel: ticket opened 🎫,
claimed 🙋 / released 🙌, renamed ✏️, panel switched 🔀, and closed 📪.

The log channel also carries a ⚠️ capacity warning when a ticket category comes
within 5 channels of Discord's 50-per-category cap — see
[category capacity](/docs/configuration#category-capacity).

It also records a 🛎️ line naming the [on-call staff](/docs/on-call) reached when
a ticket opened — and anyone the bot couldn't DM.

Everything written here is also kept in the dashboard's
[audit log](/docs/audit-log), which is filterable, survives a channel purge, and
additionally covers configuration changes made in the dashboard.
